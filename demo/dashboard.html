Overall Trend
Prostate cancer incidence fell sharply 2000–2014, coinciding with shifts in PSA screening practices after 2012 USPSTF recommendations. Since 2015, rates have begun to stabilize and slightly increase, while mortality has remained relatively flat.

Distant Stage Rise
The most striking trend is the sustained increase in distant-stage diagnoses (metastatic at diagnosis) since 2008—up nearly 50% over the period. This may reflect shifting screening practices, but also warrants attention to late-stage detection rates in vulnerable populations.

Significant Health Disparities
Non-Hispanic Black men have both the highest incidence (175.2/100K) and mortality (37.9/100K) rates. Disparities reflect complex interactions of biology, screening access, healthcare quality, socioeconomic factors, and systemic inequities. Targeted screening and early intervention in high-burden communities is critical.

Regional Patterns
Incidence rates vary substantially across states (72–149 per 100,000), with higher rates in the Southeast and Mid-Atlantic regions. These differences may reflect variation in screening practices, demographic composition, healthcare access, and surveillance completeness.

Early Detection Saves Lives
Nearly 100% of men diagnosed with localized prostate cancer survive 5+ years. In contrast, those with distant (metastatic) disease at diagnosis have only 38% survival. This stark difference underscores the critical importance of early detection and screening in eligible populations.

Screening Recommendations
For average-risk men: Discuss PSA screening at age 55-69. Decision should be individualized after discussion with your healthcare provider about benefits and risks.
Risk Factors
Age (risk increases after 65), race/ethnicity (Black men have higher risk), family history (first-degree relative with prostate cancer), and obesity may increase risk.
Take Action
Talk to your healthcare provider about your individual risk. Screening decisions should balance early detection benefits against potential harms like false positives and overtreatment.

Data Sources & Methods

SEER Cancer Statistics
Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) Program of the National Cancer Institute (NCI). SEER database covers ~35% of the US population and provides age-adjusted incidence and mortality rates with high quality control.
https://seer.cancer.gov/
USPSTF Prostate Cancer Screening
US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) guidelines on PSA screening recommendations. 2012 recommendation against routine screening, 2018 update recommending individualized decision-making for ages 55-69.
https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/
National Cancer Institute
Comprehensive cancer information and statistics from the National Cancer Institute, part of the National Institutes of Health (NIH).
https://www.cancer.gov/
Methodology Notes
All rates are age-adjusted to the 2000 US standard population. Projections 2023–2030 use simple linear regression on the last 8 years of data with 95% confidence bands. Disparities analysis focuses on race/ethnicity and geographic variation.
Dashboard Technical Details
Interactive dashboard built with Plotly.js. All data is embedded as JavaScript objects; no server backend required. Charts are fully responsive and support interactive exploration (zoom, pan, hover).
How to Use This Dashboard
Navigate between tabs to explore different aspects of prostate cancer epidemiology. Use the "Your Risk" tab to learn about personalized risk based on age and race. Download any chart as PNG using the camera icon in the top right.
